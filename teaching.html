Teaching, Mentoring & Outreach

I want students to experience science the way researchers do: through curiosity, evidence, and interpretation. Whether I'm teaching a lab, leading a field trip, or mentoring a student through their first research project, I try to give students room to wrestle with real data, ask questions, and learn from mistakes, while making sure working independently never means struggling alone. My favorite moments as a teacher are when students see the world differently, take ownership of what they discover, and begin to think like scientists.

Classroom & field teaching

Summers 2021 & 2022 · UT Austin

Ecological Niche Modeling Workshop

I developed and taught an introductory ecological niche modeling workshop twice for high school students through the Jackson School of Geosciences Research Traineeship Experience. Many students had little or no prior programming experience, so I designed approachable code and activities that invited them to explore how the models worked.

Students changed models themselves, tested assumptions, and examined how their decisions altered predictions of where species could live. I wanted computational tools to become something students could investigate and question, not just black boxes that produced answers.

Teaching approach

Learn by changing the model

Students gain confidence when they can manipulate a model, look critically at its output, and explain why a prediction changed.

Fall 2024 · UT Austin · Earth and Planetary Processes Through Time

Pleistocene Texas: Fossil Lecture & Lab

I designed and taught a week of lectures and laboratory activities focused on the Pleistocene fossil record of Texas. In the laboratory, students examined unsorted cave sediment with handheld microscopes, separated fossils from sediment, and worked through the challenges of identification.

Students compared their finds, defended their interpretations, and considered what small fossil fragments reveal about past environments. In lecture, specimens such as saber-toothed cat skulls and mammoth molars helped bring Ice Age ecosystems into the classroom.

UT Austin · Sedimentary field instruction

Guadalupe Mountains Field Trip

Field teaching lets students move between seeing a geological feature and interpreting the processes that formed it. During a field trip to the Guadalupe Mountains as part of sedimentary coursework, I guided students through field observations, stratigraphic interpretation, and discussion of ancient reefs.

At one overlook, students practiced sketching the landscape and separating observations from interpretation. Elsewhere, their realization that there were “crinoids everywhere!” opened a conversation about Permian reef ecology and the organisms that built those reefs.

Research mentoring

Undergraduate research · 2022–present

Undergraduate Research Mentorship

I mentor undergraduate researchers working on questions in reef ecology, conservation, water quality, and functional traits. My approach emphasizes developing research questions, building confidence with unfamiliar methods, and learning to interpret results critically.

Students I have mentored have contributed to research on coral functional traits, water quality in northeast Jamaica, and the adaptation of educational activities for Jamaican communities. I especially value seeing students develop increasing independence and contribute their own ideas to ongoing research.

Mentoring philosophy

Independence with support

I encourage students to make decisions, investigate unexpected results, and take ownership of their research while knowing they can ask questions and get support.

2024–2025 · Northeast Jamaica

GEOPAths GO! Jamaica

GEOPAths GO! Jamaica is an NSF-supported program bringing undergraduate and graduate students, researchers, and local partners together to investigate conservation questions in Jamaican coastal ecosystems. Its projects connect biology, geology, chemistry, oceanography, and paleontology through field-based research.

I helped organize and facilitate the field program, developed daily research activities, and mentored students through field collection, data analysis, and the presentation of results. Projects included investigating Pleistocene coral reefs and examining river and coastal water chemistry, including intensive diel sampling.

One of my favorite parts of the program was helping students move from learning research methods to asking and answering their own questions. Alongside their scientific work, students participated in conservation activities with local partners.

Community outreach

Summer 2022 · Negril, Jamaica

Negril STEM Camp

I participated in a STEM camp for Jamaican primary-school students, teaching alongside educators and other instructors. I developed and led two coral reef activities: an interactive marine food-web lesson and an adaptation of the Reef Survivor educational game.

Working with a high school teacher, I adapted the lessons for students with different levels of background knowledge. We incorporated vocabulary support, hands-on activities, and several ways to participate. Students explored ecological relationships and how environmental changes can move through a reef food web.

2023–2024 · UT Austin

Art Your Science

I helped organize and coordinate Art Your Science exhibitions at the University of Texas at Austin on multiple occasions. The exhibitions brought together artwork created by scientists and science-inspired art from the Jackson School of Geosciences community.

Art offers another way to begin conversations about science. I value spaces where people can encounter research through images, objects, and personal interpretations and engage with questions they might not meet in a classroom or research talk.
